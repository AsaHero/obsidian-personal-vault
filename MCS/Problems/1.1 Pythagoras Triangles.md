The Pythagorean Theorem says that if a and b are the lengths of the sides of a right triangle, and c is the length of its hypotenuse, then:
$$a^2+b^2=c^2$$
## Proof without words

Here’s the strategy. Suppose you are given four different colored copies of a right triangle with sides of lengths a, b and c, along with a suitably sized square, as shown:

![[Pasted image 20261010200434.png]]

**(a) Cut four triangles out of paper or draw them**. 
Place them inside a c × c square so each triangle’s **hypotenuse lies along one outer edge** of the big square. Rotate each one 90° from the previous. A square-shaped hole appears in the middle, and that’s where the small square goes.

To see why the hole has side b − a, look along one edge of the hole. It’s part of one triangle’s long leg (length b), and the next triangle’s short leg (length a) covers the rest of that leg.

**(b) Make an a × a square and a b × b square.**  
Put an a × a square and a b × b square side by side, touching. Now try to show that this combined shape can be cut into exactly the same five pieces: four triangles plus the (b − a) square. Experiment with paper here; it clicks much faster than staring at it.

After that, the conclusion follows: same pieces means same total area, so **c² = a² + b²**. [[pythagoras-rearrangement.html|Open the animation]]
