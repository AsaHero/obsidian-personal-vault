An equation connects two [[Expressions#Expressions|expressions]], illustrating how the variables involved are related and allowing us to determine their values.

An **equation** is a statement that asserts the equality of two [[Expressions#Expressions|expressions]], indicating that they have the same value. It consists of two expressions separated by an equals sign (=), which signifies that both expressions yield the same result. Let's consider some examples:

![[Pasted image 20240223022336.png]]

In the picture above, we have an unknown factor on the scale, represented by a square with an unknown weight. The balance of the scale indicates that both sides have the same weight. Thus, we can denote the square by $s$. *When we add 10 to $s$, it equals 40.*

$$s + 10 = 40$$

Solving equations typically aims to find the value(s) of the variable(s) that make the equation true. This process involves manipulating the equation by applying mathematical operations equally to both sides to maintain the balance of equality. In the given example, for the equation $s + 10 = 40$, we solve for $s$ by subtracting 10 from both sides, yielding $s = 30$. To verify our solution, we substitute the found value back into the equation: $30 + 10 = 40$, confirming the solution is correct. Thus, the equation is solved.

## Working Backward
___

Equations are made of [[Expressions#Variables|variables]], [[Expressions#Constant|constants]], and [[Expressions#Operations|operations]].
To solve an equation, we can undo the operations.

When solving an equation, the goal is to find a specific value of the variable that makes the equation true. To accomplish this, we can undo each operation and write a new, still true, equation.

$$
\displaylines{3x + 9 = 42\\3x + 9 - 9 = 42 - 9\\3x = 33\\ \frac{3x}{3} = \frac{33}{3}\\ x = 11} 
$$
As you can see, we undoing every operation in each side of the equation to keep it true, and coming to the solution.

## Distributing and Factoring

The **distributive property** says that $a(b+c)$ and $ab+ac$ are equivalent. We can also say to distribute a variable or expression.   

Said another way, this equation is true, no matter what numbers are used for $a$,$b$,and $c$:

$$
a(b+c) = ab+ac
$$
