In mathematics, a relationship between two [[Expressions|expressions]] or values that are not equal to each other is called **inequality**. So, a lack of balance results in inequality. For example, if you want to buy a new bicycle that costs 250, but you have 225. It is also an inequality as you are comparing two numbers that aren’t equal.

We use ‘=’ when two quantities are equal and when they are not equal we use the symbol ≠ to denote not equal. If two things are not equal, the first value can be either greater than (>) or lesser than (<) or greater than equal to (≥) or less than equal to (≤) the second value. So, as per the above example, 250>225.
#### Graphing Simple Inequalities

Let's solve a problem, using inequality:

1. The Evilcorp food lab is developing a new high-protein snack. The key ingredient needs to be stored at less than 5° C. Below Inequality represents all the temperatures,t, at which the ingredient can be stored. 
$$t < 5$$
A **solution to an inequality** is a value that makes the inequality true.

For example, for $t<5$:
- −7 **is** a solution because $−7<5$ is true.

- 13 **isn't** a solution because $13<5$ isn't true.
   
- 5 **isn't** a solution because $5<5$ isn't true.

The inequality $t<5$ has many, many solutions. In fact, it would be impossible to list them all.

2. Evilcorp has to meet some more requirements for the new snack. In order to qualify as high-protein, each serving of the snack needs **at least** 40 **grams** of protein.

Notice that when the amount of protein is **at least** 40 grams, it could be greater than **or equal to** 40.

The symbol for “greater than or equal to” is $≥$. So the mathematical representation of snack requirements: 

$$p≥40$$

> [! Boundary Value]
> When writing inequalities, we need to specify whether the solution includes the **boundary value**: 
> - $x≥40$ means “x is greater than or equal to 40,” and the boundary value, 40, is included in the solution.
> - $x>40$ means “x is greater than 40,” and the boundary value, 40, is **not** included in the solution.
