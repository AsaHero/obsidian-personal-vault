The **slope** of a line is the ratio of the change in $y$ to the change in $x$. It describes the steepness of the line. Here is the formula: 
$$\frac{change \space in \space y}{change \space in \space x} = slope$$
To calculate the $change\space in\space y/x$  you should take two points from related to the graph. Let's consider this [[Equations|equation]] graph: 
$$y = 2x$$

Let's take this points to test:
$P_1 = (1;2)$
$P_{2} = (2;4)$

In mathematics, change is often quantified as the difference between values over intervals. This can be represented using the delta symbol 
($Δ$/$\delta$), such as $Δx$ or $Δy$, to denote the change in variable $x$ or $y$, respectively. To find change of variable find differences  between these points we should $y_{2} - y_{1}$. And this expression can be represented with delta sign in math: 

> [! Change in math ?]
> $$\delta y = y_{2}-y_{1}$$

So, back to equation, let's find slope:
$$\displaylines{
Δy = 4 - 2 = 2\\
\\
Δx = 2 - 1 = 1 \\
\\
Slope=\frac{Δy}{Δx} = \frac{2}{1} = 2
}$$
As you can see result is equal to coefficient of x in the equation. So we can identify the steepness by calculating the slope of the line.   We label the slope as $m$:
$$y = mx$$
