Like equations, we can solve inequalities to see which values make the inequality true.

In general, all the rules used to solve [[Equations|equations]] are applicable in inequalities.To **solve an inequality** we write a statement that compares a variable to a number.The statement expresses all the numbers that make the inequality true. For example:

$$s + 5 < 10$$
The result is s < 5, let's check by substituting numbers less than 5:

- Substituting 4
$$4+5 < 10$$
- Substituting 1
$$1+5< 10$$

But, we should be careful with signs direction, because it can change in some cases. For example:

$$\displaylines{50<2x\\25<x\\x > 20}$$

#### Testing Values

This is one of the effective methods of solving inequalities. Let's take an inequality for sake of explanation:
$$-2x + 10 > 16$$
**Step 1.** Find [[Inequalities#^7d4ccb|boundary value]]: 

We can find the boundary value for an inequality by solving its **related equation**.
$$\displaylines{
-2x + 10 = 16 \\
-2x = 6 \\
x = -3
}$$
The boundary value is equal to -3.

**Step 2.** Test the numbers:

To identify the direction of the sign, we substitute numbers less than boundary value, and check the truth of the inequality. After, substitute numbers more than boundary value and test the truth of inequality. 

Let's peak 0 and -4 and check:

$$\displaylines{
-2 \times 0 + 10 > 16 \\
10 > 16
}$$
When numbers more than -3, the inequality is false.
$$\displaylines{-2 \times -4 + 10 > 16\\ 8+10>16\\18>16}$$
As you can see, numbers less than -3 makes the inequality true.
So the solution is $x < -3$.
